**TL;DR:** Each verified credit has its own project, vintage and serial number, which is naturally a job for an ERC-721 (NFT). Trading is easier with interchangeable units, which is a job for ERC-20. Carbonized lets you hold the unique credit as an NFT and wrap it one-to-one into a pooled ERC-20 for trading, then unwrap it again. Retirement happens on the NFT.

"Should carbon credits be NFTs?" is a question that usually triggers either hype or eye-rolling. The honest answer is: it depends on which property you care about at which step. Carbonized uses both token standards, each for what it does well.

## Why credits are not naturally fungible

Two credits may both be one tonne of CO2e, but they can differ in ways buyers care about:

- **Methodology:** Verra VCS, Gold Standard, or another programme
- **Project type:** removal, avoidance, nature-based, engineered
- **Vintage:** the year of the reduction
- **Geography and co-benefits**
- **Serial number:** the identifier in the issuing registry

A unique token per credit preserves all of that. It also gives you something valuable for integrity: a one-of-a-kind object cannot be accidentally merged with another. This is the same reason registries assign serials in the first place.

## What ERC-721 gives you

ERC-721 is the non-fungible standard. Every token has a unique ID and exactly one owner.

- **Metadata travels with the token.** Carbonized stores the project, methodology, vintage, tonnes, serial and an oracle proof hash in the credit record.
- **Uniqueness is enforceable.** The contract hashes the canonical serial number (trimmed and upper-cased; the methodology label is not part of the key) and refuses to mint the same serial twice, a direct defence against double issuance. See [double counting](/blog/double-counting-and-on-chain-registries).
- **Retirement is clean.** Burning one specific token while storing a certificate tells a precise story: this credit, for this reason, by this wallet.

The cost of ERC-721 for trading is friction. A buyer who wants 500 tonnes would need to buy many individual items, each with its own price. Liquidity fragments into thousands of one-off listings.

## What ERC-20 gives you

ERC-20 is the fungible standard: balances, not individual items. Any one unit equals any other.

- **Divisibility.** Trade 0.37 tonnes if you wish.
- **Simple pricing.** A single price per unit across a pool makes order books and automated market makers practical.
- **Composability.** Wallets, exchanges and other contracts already understand ERC-20.

The cost of pure ERC-20 is the loss of identity. If you pour all credits into one fungible token without discipline, a low-quality credit and a high-quality one become indistinguishable, and the market tends to price at the quality of the worst unit. That is the risk of any commodity pool.

## How Carbonized combines them

Carbonized's flow is deliberately layered:

1. **Mint:** an authorised issuer mints a `CarbonCreditNFT` for a reduction it reports. The contract checks only that the serial is new and non-empty and that the oracle proof hash is non-zero; it does not independently verify the reduction.
2. **Wrap:** the owner calls `wrapCredit(tokenId)`. The NFT is locked in the token contract, and Carbon Credit Token (CCT) is minted equal to the NFT's tonnes. Amounts use 18 decimals, so `1e18` units is one tonne.
3. **Trade:** CCT trades against a payment token on the marketplace through an order book and auctions.
4. **Unwrap or retire:** burning CCT via `unwrapCredit` returns an NFT; only the NFT can be retired onchain.

See the contract references for [CarbonCreditNFT](/docs/contracts/carbon-credit-nft) and [CarbonCreditToken](/docs/contracts/carbon-credit-token), and the [architecture overview](/docs/architecture).

### One pool per methodology

To avoid the "worst unit sets the price" trap, there is one CCT contract per methodology pool, for example `VERRA_VCS` and `GOLD_STANDARD`. A pool accepts only NFTs of its own methodology; wrapping a mismatched credit reverts. This keeps each pool's quality definition coherent, although inside a pool vintage and project differences are still averaged out. That is a real trade-off, and a more granular pool design, say by vintage band, is something worth exploring.

### Fully backed, one to one

Each CCT is backed by tonnes actually locked in NFTs. The contract exposes totals, such as tonnes wrapped and supply, so anyone can check the 1:1 relationship. Carbonized's [audit page](/docs/app/audit) reconciles event-derived totals with contract counters and shows a match or mismatch.

## Design trade-offs worth knowing

### Unwrapping returns *an* NFT, not necessarily *your* NFT

When you unwrap, you burn CCT and receive an NFT from the locked set. Depending on the function used, you may choose a specific token or take any available one. In either case, you may not get back the exact credit you deposited. That is the nature of pooled fungibility and a reason high-quality specific credits might prefer to stay as NFTs and be sold through an auction.

### Rounding and dust

Because CCT is divisible but NFTs are whole items, holders can end up with fractions of a tonne that cannot be unwrapped alone. It is a small annoyance, but real.

### Gas costs

Minting and wrapping individual NFTs costs more gas than moving ERC-20 balances. On an L2 or similar low-fee chain, this is manageable, which is a reason we target Robinhood Chain Testnet, though fees there say nothing about future mainnet conditions. See the [launch post](/blog/carb-testnet-launch-robinhood-chain).

### Retirement path

Only the NFT can be retired onchain, because the certificate belongs to a specific credit. If you hold CCT and want to claim an offset, you unwrap first. A pooled token that did not preserve provenance at retirement would be a weaker claim. The [retirement post](/blog/retirement-is-the-point) explains why.

## Alternatives we considered

- **ERC-1155 (multi-token).** It can hold both fungible and non-fungible types in one contract and supports batching. It is a credible choice, and a future migration is possible, but ERC-721 plus ERC-20 has the widest tooling support today and clear semantics.
- **Pure ERC-20 per vintage or project.** It preserves fungibility within a narrow class but multiplies the number of contracts and fragments liquidity.
- **Only NFTs plus a marketplace.** This works for high-value, low-volume credits, but not for volume trading.

## When each token is the right choice

| Goal | Better form |
| --- | --- |
| Hold a specific verified credit with full provenance | NFT |
| Sell one rare or premium credit | NFT via auction |
| Trade standardised volume quickly | CCT |
| Make a retirement claim | NFT, retired onchain |
| Provide liquidity | CCT |

## Takeaway

NFTs and ERC-20s are tools, not ideologies. Credits are unique at issuance and retirement, and fungible in between, so Carbonized models both ends with an NFT and the middle with a wrapped token. The price of that flexibility is extra complexity and some pooling trade-offs, which we try to be open about.
